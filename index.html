Kitchen Renovations Melbourne West Questions to Ask Explained

A kitchen quote only means something once both sides are describing the same job. Two renovators can price the same room far apart simply because one has allowed for demolition, service relocations and new benchtops while the other has priced doors and a splashback. That is why the sharpest kitchen renovations melbourne west questions to are not about price at all. They concern scope, drawings, contracts, trades, materials and timing. This guide arranges those questions by project stage so you can work through them in order during a measure-up or quote review. Ask these at the free measure-up

Melbourne West Kitchen Renovations describes a process that opens with a free in-home measure-up, and that first visit is your best interview opportunity. Useful questions include:

  • What does the measure-up cover, and is a written brief produced afterwards?
  • Is this room suited to a surface makeover, or does the layout justify a full rebuild? What drives that recommendation?
  • Will drawings be supplied and approved before any cabinetry is manufactured?
  • Which existing water, power or gas points would need to move, and who assesses that work?
  • In an older inner-west home, what does keeping the character mean for cabinetry, lighting and appliance placement?

The answers reveal whether you are discussing a cosmetic update or work that alters services, structure or layout, because those two jobs carry very different risks and paperwork.

Interrogate the written quote

An itemised, fixed-price written quote is the document that protects both parties, so read it line by line and ask:

  • Is the quote fixed for a defined scope, or an estimate that can drift once work starts?
  • What is excluded, and what would be treated and charged as a variation?
  • How are variations recorded, priced and approved in writing?
  • What are the payment stages, and what milestone triggers each one?
  • Are benchtop and appliance allowances shown separately so upgrades stay visible?

Variations deserve particular attention. Late changes to layout, materials or appliances are far more disruptive once a job has moved from drawings to site, so agreeing on the recording process up front saves arguments later.

Contract, insurance and licensed trades

Victorian rules give homeowners concrete checkpoints to raise before any deposit, demolition or ordering:

  • Will the project run under a written major domestic building contract? For domestic building work over $10,000, one is required under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995.
  • Does the contract value trigger domestic building insurance? Public guidance in June 2026 commonly cites more than $16,000, with reforms due from 1 July 2026, so confirm the current threshold before signing.
  • Which plumbing and electrical tasks are handled by registered trades, and what compliance paperwork accompanies them?
  • Are permits required for this scope, and who arranges them?

Because thresholds and administrative settings change, verify the current position against Victorian building guidance, and treat general renovation advice from ASIC Moneysmart as useful background reading rather than a substitute for the contract itself.

The benchtop and material questions

Material choice is now a compliance question as much as a style one. Engineered stone with 1% or more crystalline silica has been banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia since 1 July 2024. That makes these questions essential:

  • Which compliant surface is quoted: porcelain, sintered stone, natural stone, laminate, stainless steel, solid timber or a low-silica alternative?
  • How does that choice affect templating, edge detail, colour matching and splashback pairing?
  • What maintenance does the surface need over time?
  • Where is the benchtop allowance shown in the quote, and how do material and labour costs move together if the layout changes?

A quote that names a specific compliant material is far easier to compare than one that simply says stone.

Timing, disruption and handover

Published planning ranges put a standard full renovation at around 6 to 12 weeks from contract to handover, with cabinetry and benchtop fabrication often taking 4 to 6 weeks and roughly 2 to 4 weeks on site for demolition, installation, plumbing, electrical work and finishing. Those figures are guidance, not promises, so ask:

  • What happens in each phase, and on what date does the kitchen stop being usable?
  • Which selections must be locked in before fabrication begins?
  • Does the programme show measure-up, design, quote, build, install and handover stages?
  • What warranty and compliance documents are supplied at handover?

Common questions

How many quotes should I collect before choosing a renovator? There is no fixed number. The reliable order is to define the scope first, then compare itemised, fixed-price quotes for that same scope. One quote you can fully interrogate is worth more than several vague estimates covering different jobs.

Is a written contract always required for a kitchen renovation? For domestic building work over $10,000, a written major domestic building contract is required under the Domestic Building Contracts Act 1995. Domestic building insurance commonly applies above a separate threshold, cited in June 2026 guidance as more than $16,000, so confirm the current settings before signing.

Can I still choose engineered stone for my benchtop? Engineered stone with 1% or more crystalline silica has been banned for manufacture, supply, processing and installation across Australia since 1 July 2024. Ask instead about compliant options such as porcelain, sintered stone, natural stone, laminate, stainless steel, solid timber or low-silica alternatives.
